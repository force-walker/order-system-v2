# Accounts Payable / Supplier Bill

Purchase Result is the source for unbilled purchases. A Supplier Bill Draft snapshots its Supplier payment terms, quantity, UOM, effective purchase unit cost (`final_unit_cost`, otherwise `unit_cost`), SKU, and product description. The internal permanent number is `APB-00000001` format; Supplier invoice numbers must be unique among non-cancelled Bills for the same Supplier.

Draft creation accepts one or more unclaimed Purchase Results from the same Supplier. Supplier Bills are JPY-only until purchase currency is modeled on Purchase Results. Detail quantity and unit cost may be corrected on the Draft without changing Purchase Result or customer Invoice snapshots. A missing purchase unit cost remains `null` (not zero), its line amount remains undetermined, and finalization is blocked until every line has an explicit non-negative cost. An explicit zero cost is valid. Subtotal is the sum of determined line amounts; total is `subtotal + tax + adjustment`. Bill Date is the payment-terms base date. Due Date and payment terms are frozen after finalization.

Accounts payable is recognized only when a Bill is finalized. Draft and cancelled Bills have zero outstanding balance and no payment status; finalized Bills derive `unpaid`, `partially_paid`, or `paid` from active payments. Finalized Bills accept positive payments up to the outstanding amount. Payments are voided, never hard-deleted. Overdue status requires a finalized Bill with a positive balance and a Due Date before the Asia/Hong_Kong business date. A Bill with active payments cannot be cancelled. Cancelling an unpaid Bill releases its Purchase Results back to the unbilled candidate list.

PostgreSQL serializes claims with transaction-scoped advisory locks and trigger checks so a Purchase Result cannot be claimed by two active Bills. Supplier Invoice No checks use the same approach. These controls complement application row locks and are not Frontend-only validation.

Accounts Payable history derives payment totals, outstanding balances, payment state, overdue state, and line counts in SQL. Filtering, counting, sorting, offset, and limit are therefore applied by the database. CSV exports deliberately ignore screen pagination and export every Bill matching the active filters and sort order.
